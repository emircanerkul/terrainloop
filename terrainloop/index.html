The thesis

Looped is stacked.
A terrain underneath.

Pure ternary and 1-bit models flatten every weight onto uniform plateaus: every step has identical height, outliers are crushed, and training usually requires trillions of tokens from scratch.

TerrainLoop treats depth index i as a 4th dimension. Loop 0 keeps a warm-started 4-bit bumped terrain H₀; Loops 1 and 2 steer across those hills and slopes with 1-bit or ternary masks, turning 3 flat levels into dozens of expressive, energy-bounded values.

The system

Don’t flatten weights onto a rigid ternary plane—let a 4-bit terrain amplify every subsequent bit.

01

Bumped terrain, not flat plateaus

Multiplying 1-bit or ternary masks by 4-bit hill height H₀ and slope ∇H₀ gives peaks large expressive jumps while valleys stay quiet or skip cleanly.

02

Compact across the 4th dimension

Instead of storing three independent 9B slices (27B), deterministic 4D rotations Dᵢ(H₀) rotate channels across loop depth i at zero extra weight cost.

03

Bound energy, share the KV

Per-loop RMSMatch stops variance explosion across iterations, and cross-loop KV sharing keeps inference KV cache at the 9B size rather than expanding 3×.

Why it differs from a Looped Transformer

Same loop idea.
Four small per-run modifiers.

A standard Looped Transformer reuses 2 identical blocks on every pass. TerrainLoop pairs those 2 shared 4-bit terrain boxes with 4 small 1-bit/ternary modifier strips so every pass computes a distinct function.

Important

Looping 9B weights 3× cuts weight storage (13.5 GB INT4 → 6.75 GB) and memory-bandwidth traffic by ~2×, which directly accelerates memory-bound local decoding—while arithmetic FLOPs remain comparable to a 27B pass.

Advancing your sparse-diff intuition

One base sentence. Tiny + / 0 / - diffs.

Why store 4 separate full sentences when most concepts stay untouched (0) and only small modifiers shift meaning? Watch how a 4-bit anchor + 4 tiny ternary chips produce 4 distinct meanings—just like 4 looped neural passes:

Why believe this works in real LLMs

Three concrete proofs beyond metaphor

1. Transformer layers already evolve as small diffs: Residual networks compute h_{l+1} = h_l + f(h_l). Middle-to-late layers exhibit strong cross-layer representational similarity—meaning successive passes mostly apply rotations and sparse polarity flips, not wholly new random matrices.
2. 48 levels per loop vs. 3 in flat BitNet: Pure ternary {-1, 0, +1} has only 3 flat levels. Coupling 4 small ternary chips to a 4-bit terrain H₀ (16 levels) produces 16 × 3 = 48 terrain-scaled levels per run (16× richer), preserving outlier channels intact.
3. Bandwidth-bound laptop speed + warm start: Local decoding is bottlenecked by DRAM bandwidth, not FLOPs. Streaming 2 base + 4 tiny chips (~6.75 GB) instead of 27B INT4 (13.5 GB) cuts memory traffic ~2×, while warm-starting H₀ from a pretrained 9B checkpoint avoids training from scratch.

  1. 01

    Stabilize

    Warm-start a 1.5B–3B 4-bit terrain H₀, attach per-loop RMSNorm + Dᵢ + ternary T₁, T₂, and verify variance stability across 3 loops.

  2. 02

    Benchmark

    Test 9B × 3 TerrainLoop against 9B single-pass, flat looped 9B, BitNet b1.58, and 27B INT4 with cross-loop KV sharing enabled.

  3. 03

    Fuse

    Factor T₁, T₂ along the 4th dimension and write fused 4-bit + ternary CUDA/Metal kernels so modulated weights never materialize in FP16.
